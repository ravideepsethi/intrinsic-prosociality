# Titled Players Streamers and Observability Research Handoff

Prepared 27 October 2026. Project: Intrinsic Prosociality. This consolidates the completed audience analysis of 16 September and the recorded-TV analysis of 23 September 2026. It rechecks saved aggregate results and adds explicitly labeled arithmetic from those results; it does not rerun the underlying opportunity panel.

## 1. Findings to carry forward

**The defensible conclusion is that the granting pattern is not concentrated among the players identified by these audience proxies. The statement that titled players and streamers uniformly grant less often is too broad.**

1. **Elite-or-titled choosers grant less often overall:** 1,696 / 186,338 = **0.9102%**, versus **1.3747%** in the corresponding remainder. The difference is **−0.4645 percentage points (pp)**, clustered SE **0.0336**, 95% CI **[−0.5304, −0.3986]**. This group combines game-time rating ≥2400 with a recognized title and gives streamer membership priority. It is not a titled-only estimate.
2. **Known streamer choosers have a lower point estimate, with substantial uncertainty:** 29 / 2,492 = **1.1637%**, versus **1.3747%**. Difference **−0.2110 pp**, SE **0.3419**, 95% CI **[−0.8857, 0.4638]**. There are only **178 streamer choosers**, of whom **17** grant at least once.
3. **Titled-only accounts do not show a lower raw overall rate in these files.** Recognized-title choosers grant **345 / 19,088 = 1.8074%**. Opportunities involving a title on either side yield **579 / 33,695 = 1.7184%**. Their competitive-position rate is **491 / 12,315 = 3.9870%**, versus **2.7573%** for opportunities with no recognized title on either side. Their clearly-worse-position rate is lower: **0.3174% versus 0.4703%**. The latter state-specific figures are newly calculated from the saved disjoint overlap table, not newly estimated models.
4. **The strongest audience robustness result is the exclusion exercise.** Excluding opportunities with either player rated ≥2400, titled, a known streamer, linked to a streaming/video channel, or having positive recorded TV time leaves **34,208,788 opportunities and 455,609 grants**. The chooser-FE competitive-minus-clearly-worse gap is **2.0898 pp**, versus **2.1745 pp** before exclusion: **96.10%** of the original magnitude. In the sample with both profiles and numeric TV measures, the corresponding gap is **2.8593 versus 2.8794 pp**, retaining **99.30%**.
5. **The later controlled TV association is small and positive:** **+0.0674 pp**, SE **0.0401**, 95% CI **[−0.0112, 0.1460]**, p = **0.0928**. It does not establish a lower rate among TV-exposed accounts or a zero audience effect.

These results weaken an explanation in which observed granting, or its board-state gradient, is predominantly generated by the measured highly visible groups. They do not identify the causal effect of an audience, and they do not establish that the remaining games were unobserved.

Profiles were collected after the games: **1,305,683 returned accounts in the August 19–26, 2026 snapshot**, plus **122** from the September 11 supplement, totaling **1,305,805 distinct returned accounts**. ## 7. What was controlled for, and what was not

### 7.1 September audience fixed effects

The original and expanded audience FE models estimate, separately within each group and scope:

`100 × grant_it = chooser_intercept_i + beta × competitive_it + error_it`

Only competitive and clearly-worse observations enter. The coefficient is the within-chooser difference between these board states. With competitive and worse counts F_i and W_i, each chooser receives weight **F_i W_i / (F_i + W_i)** on their individual difference in rates. Choosers must appear in both states to identify the slope; never-granting accounts are retained if they meet that condition. Exclusion can change both the identifying choosers and their weights.

**These models control for chooser identity only.** They do not additionally control for month, speed, rating, clocks, ply, RD, volatility, draw payoff, win premium, K, Patron status or opponent identity. The separate payoff-curve specification `chooser_state_controls_k20` must not be attributed to the audience estimates.

SEs cluster by chooser. FE CR1 correction is **G/(G−1) × (N−1)/(N−G−1)** on the identifying sample; intervals use Student t with G−1 degrees of freedom. Raw rates and ratios use their metric-specific corrections. Group contrasts combine scores across shared choosers before squaring; reference degrees of freedom use the smaller group's cluster count minus one. Fewer than 30 clusters are flagged. The saved equivalence-margin columns at 0.25 and 0.50 pp are exploratory interval diagnostics, not a pre-established claim that audience groups are equivalent.

### 7.2 Coarse common-support adjustment in the original run

This separate adjustment jointly stratifies on **both ratings in 200-point bins, speed, calendar quarter, ply (≤10, 11–30, 31–60, >60), and each clock (<15, 15–<60, ≥60 seconds)**. It requires valid complete controls and at least **10 opportunities in each of four group × board-state cells** per retained stratum. All four cells use the same weights proportional to total stratum observations. Inference conditions on these strata and weights.

## 9. Implication for observability and manuscript wording

Three distinct empirical questions must stay separate:

| Question | Available evidence | Supported conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are conspicuously visible groups more generous on average? | Raw elite/title/streamer rates and later controlled TV association | No uniform positive relationship; elite/title union lower overall, streamer point estimate lower but imprecise, title-only and channel links higher overall |
| Could the aggregate pattern be driven by those groups? | Cumulative either-player exclusions, repeated within observed metadata | The pattern remains large after removing measured visible groups; they do not account for most grants or most of the estimated board-state gap |
| Does actual observation causally change granting? | No focal-game spectator count, streaming state or exposure design | Not identified by these analyses |

Low observed rates among the elite union can challenge a simple audience-amplification account, but skill, stakes, time controls, opportunity composition and profile selection also vary. Conversely, the higher titled-only raw competitive rate cannot establish an audience motive. The exclusion result is especially useful because it directly shows that the board-state pattern extends far beyond the measured visible groups.

**Suggested manuscript paragraph, explicitly tied to this archived sample:**

> In the September robustness sample, granting was not concentrated among players with observable indicators of a potential audience. Opportunities involving either a player rated at least 2400, a recognized title holder, or a known streamer accounted for 0.57% of opportunities and 0.43% of grants. Extending this screen to profile channel links and positive recorded Lichess TV time removed 17.64% of opportunities and 20.08% of grants. Among the remaining 34.21 million opportunities, the within-chooser difference in granting between competitive and clearly worse recipient positions was 2.090 percentage points, compared with 2.174 points in the full sample. Restricting to observations with both players' profiles and numeric TV measurements gave corresponding estimates of 2.859 and 2.879 points. These findings show that the granting pattern extends beyond the groups identified by our audience proxies, although the proxies do not measure observation of the focal decision.

If discussing subgroup levels, state the elite-or-title union definition and the streamer uncertainty. Do not write “titled players grant less” as a summary of these data. Do not describe the audience FE results as controlling for draw payoff or as an estimate of the causal effect of observability.
