# Remaining dynamics completion analyses

## Status and scope

This module closes two dynamics computations promised by the analysis plan but
not produced by the certified core and second-wave runs:

1. A1 censoring at the next qualifying disconnection, together with later-
   exposure diagnostics; and
2. the A2 within-account pre/post mechanism decomposition.

Both analyses are post-outcome and secondary. They do not alter the three core
tests, the four-slot second-wave Holm family, or any E1/F2 gate decision. The
original A1 estimate remains the primary receipt-side result.

## A1: censoring at the next qualifying exposure

The sensitivity excludes a recipient's first subsequent fair choice whenever
that choice occurred after another qualifying disconnection. It therefore asks
whether the A1 association survives when the measured response is not
contaminated by a new qualifying receipt-side event.

The restriction removes 388,151 of 1,029,558 conditional-choice rows and retains
641,407 rows (62.30 percent). The total-path estimate is 1.267 percentage points
(SE 0.160; raw p-value 2.14e-15). The state-conditioned estimate is 1.270
percentage points (SE 0.159; raw p-value 1.57e-15). Both exceed the original A1
estimate of approximately 1.00 percentage point.

This sensitivity materially weakens the concern that A1 is generated by another
qualifying disconnection occurring before the observed response. It does not,
by itself, turn the observational assignment mechanism into literal
randomization.

### Later-exposure paths

Within 90 days, initial mercy receipt does not predict either:

- any later qualifying disconnection (-0.119 percentage points; p = 0.547); or
- the number of later qualifying disconnections (coefficient 0.003; p = 0.833).

Initial mercy recipients are slightly more likely to receive mercy again:

- any later mercy receipt: +0.223 percentage points (p = 0.011); and
- number of later mercy receipts: +0.0028 (p = 0.008).

The latter pattern may reflect persistent recipient circumstances or matching.
It cannot account for the censored A1 estimate because that estimate excludes a
response observed after a new qualifying disconnection.

## A2: within-account mercy-versus-claim trajectories

For each account, the analysis compares the rate of kind choices in symmetric
30-, 60-, and 90-day windows before and after the first qualifying
disconnection. It reports separate mercy-received and claimed-against paths and
their difference in changes on the frozen common-support sample. The comparison
uses the frozen ATT-style exposure-cell weights, exposure-cell and month fixed
effects, exposure controls, and exposure-chooser clustered standard errors.

With at least one fair opportunity on each side of the event, the adjusted
mercy-minus-claim differences are:

| Symmetric window | Difference in changes | Standard error | Raw p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 30 days | +1.117 pp | 0.282 pp | 0.000073 |
| 60 days | +0.754 pp | 0.227 pp | 0.000915 |
| 90 days | +0.667 pp | 0.203 pp | 0.001023 |

The broad-sample pattern is driven principally by a decline in kindness after
being claimed against, while the mercy-received path is approximately flat.

The minimum-four-opportunities-per-side sensitivity is not supportive. Its
mercy samples are small (219, 567, and 899 accounts across the three windows),
and the differences are statistically null and inconsistent in sign. The A2
pattern should therefore be described as suggestive and sample-sensitive, not
as a causal or headline result.

## Reporting decision

- Put the A1 next-exposure censoring estimate in the main dynamics discussion
  or its principal robustness table.
- Put the later-exposure paths in the appendix as identification diagnostics.
- Put A2 in the appendix or in a clearly labelled exploratory mechanism
  subsection. Do not make it a new pillar of the paper.
